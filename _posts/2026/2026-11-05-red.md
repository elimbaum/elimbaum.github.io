Earlier this year I bought some [Harman Red 125](https://www.harmanphoto.co.uk/harman-red-125-35mm). It gives a nearly-monochromatic, warm-yet-apocalyptic image. But since it is still color film, other hues sometimes leak through. Apparently, this is the effect you can get by shooting through C-41 film backwards: most of the light is absorbed by the red layer, and not transmitted.

![home](/assets/img/film26red/home.jpeg)
_A bit of green leaks through_

Due to the unusual color, some scanning equipment has trouble handling these negatives, so the initial scans back from the lab were washed out. I redid these on my grandfather's 25-year-old Epson _Perfection 2450_ flatbed, which is still chugging along.

![paulina](/assets/img/film26red/paulina.jpeg)

The negatives come out a vivid blue:

![negatives](/assets/img/film26red/negative.jpeg)

I was still observing a light leak on many of these shots (the bright vertical stripes). Above, you can also see the leak on the sprocket holes — I think those dark regions are where light spilled onto the right-hand spindle and shone through some of the outer sprocket holes. However, I wasn't really able to correlate this theory with the geometry of the camera. Some electrical tape on the hinge has seemed to help since then.
